LocalNeuro is a small language model that runs entirely on your own computer. It does not need a graphics card, a fast internet connection, or a remote server. The whole system is small enough to read in an afternoon and light enough to train on a modest laptop.

A language model learns to predict the next piece of text from the text that came before it. When you read the first half of a sentence, you can often guess how it ends. The model turns that same guessing game into numbers, and it improves a little every time it sees more writing.

The model is built from simple parts. Text is split into small tokens. Each token becomes a vector of numbers. The vectors pass through layers of attention, and attention lets every token look back at the tokens before it. At the end the model produces a score for every possible next token.

Training is a long loop. The model reads a batch of text, makes a prediction, and measures how wrong it was. A small correction is then applied to every weight. After many thousands of steps the predictions grow sharp, and the loss curve flattens out near the bottom.

Attention is the heart of the model. For every token the model forms a question, and for every earlier token it forms a key and a value. The question is compared with each key, the best matches are kept, and their values are blended together. In this way a token gathers the context it needs.

A tokenizer is the bridge between letters and numbers. It begins with raw bytes and learns which pairs of bytes appear together most often. Those pairs are merged into longer tokens, again and again, until the vocabulary is full. Common words become single tokens, while rare words are still spelled out in pieces.

Memory is the main limit on a small machine. Weights, gradients, and activations all compete for the same space. Quantization helps by storing each weight as a small integer instead of a wide floating point number. The model becomes lighter, and a larger network can fit in the same budget.
